Pathway of Sacred Unity and Learning

Dar-ul-Hadees Ahmadiya Salafiah Madrasah ComplexA 7-acre campus where learning, worship and public life share one connected ground.

Integrating Urban Amenities with Transit Stops

ConceptBridging space and people through TOD.

  • Bridging movement. The transit system becomes the connecting element between urban functions, linking transport, public space and community amenities.
  • Connecting people and places. Pedestrian paths, plazas, bridges and landscaped areas run continuously from the stops into the surrounding spaces, so people walk, gather and meet.
  • Transit as urban life. The station is integrated with public amenities, green space, water features and community spaces instead of standing as isolated infrastructure.
  • People-centred public space. The areas around the stops become active, welcoming places for everyday movement and social life.

A Pet-Friendly Space in Katabon: A Bidirectional Bond

ConceptLiving, learning and growing together.

  • Human–animal connection. A pet-friendly urban environment that encourages positive interaction between people and animals through shared spaces and activities.
  • Living together. Pet areas, shade, water features, seating and planted zones make the space comfortable for people and pets alike.
  • Learning and interaction. Spaces for socializing, observing and learning bring the community together around the animals.
  • Connected public space. A continuous network joins the activity areas and invites walking, movement and exploration.
  • Nature and well-being. Trees, water and open ground make a healthier, more engaging everyday environment.
  • Inclusive community. The design asks how public space can support social contact, accessibility and a stronger sense of community.

Reviving Benarasi Palli at Mirpur

Economic regeneration of Benarasi PalliA denser, healthier neighbourhood that keeps its weavers and their craft at the centre.

  • Regeneration without displacement. Reorganized a dense settlement into a healthier, better-connected environment while keeping the existing community and its tradition of Benarasi weaving.
  • Living and working together. Residences, weaving workshops, training spaces, markets, schools, mosques and shared gathering spaces sit side by side, tying everyday life to the local economy.
  • Streets and nodes. Connected pedestrian streets meet at communal nodes with shops, shade trees and seating.
  • Healthier ground. Green buffers, open spaces and improved ventilation, lighting and drainage support movement, interaction and neighbourhood well-being.
